1. PHOTON BUDGET: why a 10 GHz single-photon link cannot work
==============================================================================
carrier                 f (Hz)    E=hf (J)   kT/E @290K  n_thermal
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1 GHz microwave      1.000e+09  6.6261e-25       6042.6   6.04e+03
10 GHz microwave     1.000e+10  6.6261e-24        604.3        604
1550 nm optical      1.934e+14  1.2816e-19          0.0   1.26e-14

optical / microwave photon energy ratio : 19,341
  -> the discussion said '~100,000x less'. That is the 1-2 GHz number.
  -> at 10 GHz the honest ratio is ~19,341x.

Single mode at 290 K, 10 GHz, holds ~604 thermal photons.
  A 'single photon' detector must resolve 1 photon inside a 600-photon
  bath: that is the wall. Hence CV-QKD (which measures quadratures
  relative to a strong local oscillator) instead of photon counting.

2. BB84 WITH INTERCEPT-RESEND EVE: the 25% error signature
==============================================================================
scenario                             sifted   errors     QBER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
clean channel (no eavesdropper)        9991        0   0.00%
intercept-resend Eve (full tap)        9991     2481  24.83%

Theory: Eve guesses the basis correctly 1/2 of the time; when she
guesses wrong, Bob is wrong 1/2 of the time -> QBER = 1/4 = 25.00%.

Security threshold (one-way, asymptotic) : 11.0%
Measured QBER                            : 24.83%
Verdict                                  : ABORT KEY
Secret fraction 1 - 2*H2(Q)              : -0.6172
  -> negative: at Q > 11% no key survives privacy amplification.
  The tap is detected, not ignored -- this is the whole point.

3. CV-QKD EXCESS-NOISE CHECK (toy model, not a security proof)
==============================================================================
Alice modulation variance V_A = 4.0 SNU, transmittance T = 0.5

channel                                   xi_hat +/- SE       verdict
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
clean-ish link, xi = 0.02                 0.0156 +/-0.0064      ACCEPT
Eve taps, +0.30 SNU of noise              0.6143 +/-0.0083       ABORT

  The sampling noise on the clean case (~0.006 SNU) is why 0.02
  estimates as ~0.016: the estimator is unbiased, just finite-sample.

Abort threshold used here: xi_max = 0.1 SNU (illustrative; real
thresholds follow from a composable secret-key-rate analysis and
depend on T, reconciliation efficiency and V_A).

MICROWAVE REALITY CHECK (same estimator, thermal bath as 'noise'):
  thermal occupation @290 K, 10 GHz : 604 SNU  <- vs xi_max 0.1
  thermal occupation @  4 K, 10 GHz : 7.84e+00 SNU
  factor by which 290 K noise exceeds the abort threshold : 6,038x

  -> room-temperature CV-QKD at 10 GHz is off by ~3 orders of
     magnitude on noise alone. Practical routes: cryogenic receivers,
     near-quantum-limited LNAs, or optical upconversion of the
     microwave signal before detection. The discussion's framing of
     'I/Q quadratures = QAM plane' is right; the 'room temperature'
     implication is the part that needs the qualifier.

4. PHASED-ARRAY STEERING: dphi = 2*pi*d*sin(theta)/lambda
==============================================================================
f = 10 GHz   lambda = 3.00 cm   d = lambda/2 = 1.50 cm

(a) required inter-element phase step
   theta  sin(theta)        dphi   dphi mod 360
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
      0deg      0.0000       0.00          0.00
     10deg      0.1736      31.26         31.26
     20deg      0.3420      61.56         61.56

     90deg      1.0000     180.00        180.00

Check the worked case from the discussion: theta = 30 deg
  dphi = pi*sin(30) = pi/2 = 90.00 deg  -> 90 deg. OK

(b) numeric pattern peak for a 16-element array, commanded theta_0 = 30 deg
  predicted peak : 30.000 deg
  measured peak  : 30.000 deg   (AF = 1.0000)
  error          : 0.0000 deg  -> formula confirmed

(c) 100-element array beamwidth
  -3 dB width    : 1.173 deg
  theory 0.886*lambda/(N*d) broadside = 1.015 deg
  broadened by 1/cos(30 deg) at scan  = 1.172 deg   -> 0.1% off
  -> a ~1 deg pencil beam: an enemy 3 deg off-axis sits -20.4 dB down.

(d) finite cyclic phase groups: quantization of the steering step

------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  At theta_0 = 30 deg with d = lambda/2 the step is exactly 90 deg,
  so C4 and everything above it is exact. That is a coincidence of
  this angle -- not a general property. See theta_0 = 17 deg below.

  same test at theta_0 = 17.0 deg (dphi = 52.627 deg):
  group      dphi_q   theta_q  point err  scan loss

(e) the modulo-360 wraparound the hardware actually stores
  commanded phases for elements 0..11 (degrees, mod 360):
    0.0  270.0  180.0   90.0    0.0  270.0  180.0   90.0    0.0  270.0  180.0   90.0
  distinct values needed for this beam: 4  -> the pattern just repeats.

  Sign note: this array applies -n*dphi, so the sequence runs
  0, 270, 180, 90, 0, ... because a POSITIVE dphi steers to +theta
  under this convention. Writing +n*dphi (0, 90, 180, 270) is the
  same beam mirrored to -theta. Both are convention, not error --
  but the sign of dphi is what picks left from right in hardware.

(f) element spacing and grating lobes (theta_0 = 30 deg, N = 16)
    the phase step is re-solved for each spacing, so the main beam
    stays commanded to 30 deg and spacing is the only variable
  d/lambda                             beams found           level
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
     0.500            main +30.0deg, side +18.7deg   -13.1 dB side
     0.667            equal beams at -88deg/+30deg     0.0 dB each
     0.700            equal beams at -68deg/+30deg     0.0 dB each
     1.000            equal beams at -30deg/+30deg     0.0 dB each

  No-grating-lobe condition: d/lambda <= 1/(1+|sin(theta_0)|).
  For theta_0 = 30 deg that bound is 1/(1+0.5) = 0.667: d = lambda/2
  sits 25% below it. At d = lambda the image lobe lands at exactly
  -30 deg, a full-strength copy of your beam aimed at the enemy. That,
  not aesthetics, is why tactical arrays use lambda/2.
  (At d = lambda/2 the ~-13 dB lobes are ordinary uniform-array
  sidelobes, not grating lobes; tapering the amplitude flattens them.)
